How a round is limited is on [Painting](/painting). What the pixel score does and does not say is on [Accuracy](/accuracy). Online and local party play is on [Multiplayer](/multiplayer).

## What Steam says the game is

The short description on the store:

> Sloppy Forgeries is a fast-paced competitive painting game. Practice your skills by butchering iconic masterpieces in one of the few drawing games that measures accuracy pixel-by-pixel in real-time.

The longer pitch is a list of limits. Copy the Mona Lisa with a mouse in three minutes. The store’s own reply to “that’s impossible” is yes. Then it counts what you get: 4–6 colors, three brush sizes named medium, big, and too big, no undo, no fill, and a score based on every pixel you paint.

A later paragraph says you choose from 50 meticulously abstracted paintings from art history. That is the store’s count. This guide does not turn store screenshots into a full catalog. The [painting](/painting) page says which titles are actually legible in those frames.

## Release and the free demo

The full game was still upcoming on 12 November 2026. 13 November 2026 is the planned unlock.

The same store page says a free demo is available. That was still true in the API that day. The demo is its own app, **5274180**, named Sloppy Forgeries Demo. `is_free` is true, `type` is demo, `coming_soon` is false, and `release_date` is 18 Oct 2026. Its `fullgame` field points at AppID 3030560. Platforms are Windows only. The demo’s requirement block only says a 64-bit processor and operating system. Its language string is English, marked for full audio. The full game’s language string is the separate pair English, Japanese, without that audio note.

The full-game payload’s `dlc` array also lists **5039270**. That app is named Sloppy Forgeries Playtest. Its type field is `game`, not a content pack. `coming_soon` is false, the date is 2 Oct 2026, and `is_free` is false. This page does not describe it as downloadable bonus content, and it does not invent a price for it.

## Where the rest of the guide goes

**Paint.** A round is a timed copy with a short palette, three coarse brushes, no undo, and no fill. Assist tools, a tablet note, the piano tracks, and the 50-count catalog are on [Painting](/painting).

**Score.** The store’s phrase is pixel-by-pixel, in real time, using every pixel you paint. Steam Leaderboards are listed per painting. The formula is not printed. That limit is the whole point of [Accuracy](/accuracy).

**Party.** Online uses a join code. Local uses two mice on one computer. The store’s examples of random variants are Tiny Brush, Upside Down, and Pointillism. Details and the 7-friends / 8-people wording are on [Multiplayer](/multiplayer).

Single-player lines on the store, kept here so the party page does not have to repeat them:

- A masterpiece catalog tracks forgery scores and stats across the 50 paintings.
- A painting archive stores every forgery locally.
- Time-lapse replays cover each stroke.
- Replays can be downloaded and shared as animated GIFs.
- Keyboard shortcuts exist for some actions. The store does not print the key list. The sentence jokes that it is not “everything” or “most things.”
- Wacom tablet support is listed, including two tablets at once.
- Music is described as 8 or more randomly selected fast piano pieces that end on a dramatic note. The credit line is Bernd Krueger, source piano-midi.de.
